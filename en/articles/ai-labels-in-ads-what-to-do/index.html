AI labels in ads: what they are and what to do

AINovember 19, 20265 min read

In short

Ads with AI-created or AI-edited assets can receive labels on ad platforms. In Google Ads the advertiser declares AI use and the platform may also label on its own. It pays to build an internal process to know what was AI-generated, declare it correctly and keep a record.

An AI label on an ad is a signal, shown to the audience by the ad platform, that a creative was created or edited with artificial intelligence. On Google, the advertiser can declare AI use for each asset, and the platform may also apply the label itself where required.

For anyone running paid media this is no longer theoretical: generative AI tools now live inside the platforms and, in some formats, come switched on by default. Knowing what was AI-generated and how it gets flagged has become part of day-to-day operations.

How does Google handle AI content labels?

According to the official Google Ads Editor help page, the advertiser declares that certain assets were generated or edited with AI. Those ads get a statement in the "How the ad was made" section of My Ad Center, reachable globally through the three-dot menu on Search, YouTube and Discover ads.

In some regions the help page says the label also appears on the ad itself: the European Union, India, New York, California, South Korea and Vietnam. The Demand Gen help page explains that regulations in those regions require statements and/or labels on ads with certain AI-generated or AI-edited assets.

Points the documentation highlights:

  • Who labels: the advertiser makes the declaration, but Google may also label when there is a legal requirement or signals from other platforms. Labels on assets created by Google's automated features cannot be changed.
  • Two options when declaring: mark the asset as created or edited with AI, or do not label it.
  • Digital marking: Google applies SynthID and C2PA metadata to images and videos generated in its tools.
  • Google's own warning: using the setting does not guarantee compliance with a specific regulation. When in doubt, seek legal advice.

What about Meta?

Meta's official documentation was not consulted for this piece. According to an article by Conversion (July 2026), Meta labels ads created or edited with generative AI tools on Facebook and Instagram, including third-party tools, based on C2PA metadata, with the label shown under "About this ad". Treat that as an indication and confirm in Meta's help center before building a process on it.

Where does AI come in without you asking?

In Google's Demand Gen campaigns, the official help page says several creative enhancements are checked by default in new campaigns: vertical, square and shorter video versions, auto-generated videos, adaptive layouts and landing page preview. So opening the campaign and checking which boxes are ticked is part of the process.

Step by step for your team

  1. Map where AI enters. List external tools (image and video generators) and the platforms' automatic features.
  2. Review campaign defaults. In Demand Gen campaigns, check which enhancements are ticked and untick those the brand does not approve.
  3. Set a declaration rule. Decide when an asset counts as "created or edited with AI" and who marks it.
  4. Record the origin. Note in the creative folder which tool was used and who approved it. Without a record, the declaration depends on memory.
  5. Preserve metadata. Avoid exports that strip origin information from the file.
  6. Review the output before upload. AI-generated copy, claims, faces and products need a human check.
  7. Align with legal. Google itself warns the setting does not guarantee compliance, and rules vary by region.

What about AI content on your website?

Google Search Central's guidance covers organic content, not ads, but it helps calibrate rigor: publishing many AI pages without adding value may violate the scaled content abuse spam policy, models can get facts wrong, and content should be reviewed before publishing, including titles, meta descriptions and alt text. Common mistakes

  • Assuming the platforms' AI features are off.
  • Uploading AI creatives with no origin record.
  • Stripping metadata on export.
  • Treating the label as a complete legal solution.
  • Applying one region's rule to every campaign, or the other way round.

How to tell if it is working

Run a monthly audit: sample live creatives and check that each has a recorded origin, a declaration consistent with that origin, and only the automatic enhancements the brand approved. If you cannot answer "was this made with AI?" within a minute, the process is still fragile.

Quick questions

Do I have to declare every creative made with AI?

It depends on region, format and use. Google Ads has a setting to declare assets created or edited with AI, and some regions require the label by regulation. Confirm with legal.

Can Google label my ad without my asking?

Yes. According to the official help page, Google may also label when there is a legal requirement or signals from other platforms, and labels on assets created by Google's automated features cannot be changed.

Does an AI label hurt ad performance?

The sources consulted give no data on this. The honest answer is to test with your own data.

What are SynthID and C2PA?

They are digital markings that indicate a file's origin. According to Google's help page, it applies them to images and videos generated in its ad tools.

Does this apply to website content?

The ad labeling rule is different from the guidance for organic content, which focuses on quality, review and avoiding scaled pages without value.
